TITLE
Svarloka, the Heaven of Indra: Amaravati, the Moon, Planets & the Pole Star | The Seven Worlds III

DESCRIPTION
The third world of Hindu cosmology: Svarloka, the heavens that stretch from the sun up to the Pole Star, as the Puranas describe it.

Passing through the light of the sun at the end of Part II, the soul enters heaven. It sees Amaravati, the city of Indra, and Indra himself enthroned with Shachi in his assembly, where apsaras dance and gandharvas play. It meets Airavata, the white horse Uchchaihshravas and the Parijata tree, all born from the churning of the ocean. Here the righteous enjoy the fruit of their good deeds, but only until their merit runs out. Higher still ride the Moon and the planets in their chariots, past the ring of the lunar mansions and the seven sages, to the heavenly Ganges and the starry wheel of Shishumara turning around the unmoving Pole Star, the edge of this world.

Part III of the series The Seven Worlds. Previous: "Bhuvarloka, the World Between Earth and Sun". Overview of the whole journey: "The Seven Worlds: A Soul's Journey to Brahmaloka".

SOURCES BY SCENE
• Svarloka extends from the sun to the Pole Star (Dhruva): Vishnu Purana 2.7
• Indra in his assembly with Shachi, apsaras and gandharvas: Mahabharata, Sabha Parva 7
• Airavata and the white horse Uchchaihshravas from the churning of the ocean: Bhagavata Purana 8.8.3–4
• The Parijata tree: Vishnu Purana 1.9, 5.30
• Having enjoyed heaven, when their merit is spent, they return to the world of mortals: Bhagavad Gita 9.20–21
• The Moon above the sun, the 28 lunar mansions above the Moon: Bhagavata Purana 5.22.8–11
• The Moon's three-wheeled chariot with ten white horses: Vishnu Purana 2.12
• Venus, Mercury, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn: Bhagavata Purana 5.22.12–16; their chariots: Vishnu Purana 2.12
• The seven sages circling the Pole Star: Bhagavata Purana 5.22.17
• The Ganges from the foot of Vishnu through Dhruvaloka: Bhagavata Purana 5.17.1–3
• Shishumara and Dhruva: Bhagavata Purana 5.23

NOTES
• Editions of Vishnu Purana 2.12 disagree on the number of horses of Venus and Saturn, so no number is shown for them.
• Rahu and Ketu, who also ride chariots in Vishnu Purana 2.12, are not shown.
• Uchchaihshravas is often painted with seven heads in later art; the Bhagavata Purana calls him simply a white horse, so he is shown with one.
