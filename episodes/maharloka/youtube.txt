TITLE
Maharloka, the World of the Sages: Bhrigu, the Night of Brahma & the Cosmic Ocean | The Seven Worlds IV

DESCRIPTION
The fourth world of Hindu cosmology: Maharloka, the realm of the great sages above the Pole Star, as the Puranas describe it.

Rising above Dhruva at the end of Part III, the soul enters Maharloka, where Bhrigu and the other great sages live for a whole kalpa, one day of Brahma. From here the whole cycle can be seen. A day of Brahma lasts a thousand cycles of the four ages, and his night is just as long. As night falls, seven suns scorch the earth and fire from the mouth of Sankarshana burns the three worlds. The sages, distressed by the heat, rise to Janaloka. Vast clouds pour rain for a hundred years until a single ocean covers everything up to the stars of the seven sages. On that ocean the Lord sleeps on the serpent Ananta, while the sages of Janaloka pray with folded hands. When a new day dawns, Varaha raises the Earth from the waters, and the soul follows the sages upward.

Part IV of the series The Seven Worlds. Previous: "Svarloka, the Heaven of Indra". Overview of the whole journey: "The Seven Worlds: A Soul's Journey to Brahmaloka".

SOURCES BY SCENE
• Maharloka lies above Dhruva; its inhabitants live for a kalpa: Vishnu Purana 2.7
• Maharloka, where Bhrigu and other saints live for a kalpa: Bhagavata Purana 2.2.25
• The day of Brahma is a thousand cycles of the four ages, and his night is as long; at night the three worlds disappear and the sun and moon lose their light: Bhagavata Purana 3.11.22–29
• Seven suns, the breath of Shesha, the great clouds and a hundred years of rain: Vishnu Purana 6.3
• Fire from the mouth of Sankarshana; Bhrigu and others go from Maharloka to Janaloka: Bhagavata Purana 3.11.30
• The flood fills the three worlds; the Lord lies on Ananta and the sages of Janaloka pray to Him: Bhagavata Purana 3.11.31–32
• The waters reach the region of the seven sages; Vishnu sleeps on Shesha, praised by Sanaka and the sages of Janaloka: Vishnu Purana 6.4
• At the start of the present kalpa the Lord, as a boar, lifts the Earth from the waters: Vishnu Purana 1.4

NOTES
• The Vishnu Purana describes Varaha as a boar. The painting shows him the way temple art often does, with a human body and a boar's head.
• In Vishnu Purana 6.3, Vishnu as Rudra drinks up the waters and becomes the breath of Shesha. This figure is not shown, only the seven suns and the fire.
