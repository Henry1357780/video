TITLE
Bhuvarloka, the World Between Earth and Sun: Clouds, Sages & Surya's Chariot | The Seven Worlds II

DESCRIPTION
The second world of Hindu cosmology: Bhuvarloka, the realm of air that stretches from the earth up to the sun, as the Puranas describe it.

The soul that left the body at the end of Part I rises above the Ganges, higher than the great birds fly, into the sky of winds and clouds. It passes Vayu, god of the wind, and the storm clouds of Indra. It meets the yakshas, the vidyadharas with their garlands, the singing charanas, and the siddhas and munis in meditation. Higher still come the thumb-sized Valakhilya sages chanting before the sun, the seven horses named after the Vedic meters, and the golden chariot of Surya. The journey ends in the blazing light of the sun, the boundary of this world.

Part II of the series The Seven Worlds. Previous: "Bhuloka, the Earthly World". Overview of the whole journey: "The Seven Worlds: A Soul's Journey to Brahmaloka".

SOURCES BY SCENE
• Bhuvarloka extends from the earth to the sun, the realm of siddhas and munis: Vishnu Purana 2.7
• The earth reaches as high as the great birds can fly: Bhagavata Purana 5.24.6
• Antariksha, "as far as the wind blows and the clouds float," home of the yakshas: Bhagavata Purana 5.24.5
• Vayu, and Indra the thunderer, as the deities of the middle region: Yaska, Nirukta 7.5
• The worlds of the siddhas, charanas and vidyadharas: Bhagavata Purana 5.24.4
• The seven horses named after the Vedic meters: Bhagavata Purana 5.21.15
• The 60,000 thumb-sized Valakhilya sages before the sun: Bhagavata Purana 5.21.17
• The chariot of the sun and its retinue of gandharvas and apsaras: Bhagavata Purana 5.21.12–18; Vishnu Purana 2.8–2.10
• The soul leaving through the crown of the head: Bhagavata Purana 2.2.21–22

LEFT OUT ON PURPOSE
• Bhagavata Purana 5.24.5 also places rakshasas, pishachas and ghosts in the antariksha, and 5.24.1–3 places Rahu below the sun. They are not shown, to keep the meditation calm.
• The sun's retinue (5.21.18) also includes nagas, yakshas, rakshasas and devas. Only the gandharvas and apsaras are shown.
• Aruna sits facing backward toward Surya (5.21.16). The image could not show this correctly, so he appears only in the chariot scene.
