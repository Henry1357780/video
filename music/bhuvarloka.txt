Instrumental only, absolutely no vocals, no singing, no humming. A 2.5-minute slow, airy, meditative Hindustani classical piece in raga Megh, the raga of clouds and the rainy season. Lead instrument: vichitra veena, played with long gliding slides that sound like the wind. A shimmering swarmandal (Indian harp-zither) adds sparkling arpeggios like light through clouds. Soft ambience of wind high in the sky and very distant gentle thunder. A very quiet drone underneath. No drums, no tabla, no pakhawaj, no percussion of any kind. No flute, no bansuri, no sitar, no santoor, no rudra veena, no singing bowls, no synthesizers, no electronic sounds.

[0:00-0:40] Wind and distant thunder, then the vichitra veena enters alone with slow free gliding phrases, rising upward.
[0:40-1:30] The swarmandal joins with gentle shimmering arpeggios, the melody floats among the clouds, light and spacious.
[1:30-2:10] The music grows brighter and warmer, phrases climb higher, as if approaching the radiant sun.
[2:10-2:35] A luminous final phrase on the vichitra veena dissolving into soft wind and silence.
